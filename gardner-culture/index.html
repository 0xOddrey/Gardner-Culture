Gardner Culture

Our direction

What we build today shapes who our learners become.

Our mission and vision give Gardner Culture its direction. Every belief, practice, relationship, policy, and decision should help move us towards them.

01

Gardner's Mission

To build incredibly confident and capable lifelong learners who are hilariously excited to learn every day.

Our mission describes the daily experience we create now: children developing belief in themselves, the ability to act, and genuine joy in learning.

02

Gardner's Vision

To graduate young adults who are capable of success, not just academically, but holistically.

Our vision looks beyond marks alone. Gardner graduates should be able to think, communicate, collaborate, adapt, care for themselves and others, and participate confidently in the world.

From direction to action

Culture is how we make the mission real.

  • ConfidenceChildren feel safe, seen, heard, and trusted to try.
  • CapabilityChildren practise thinking, creating, solving, communicating, and acting independently.
  • ExcitementLearning invites curiosity, participation, imagination, and discovery.
  • Holistic successAcademic, social, emotional, creative, physical, and ethical growth all matter.

01 — What we believe

Children are active participants in learning.

They bring ideas, experiences, questions, theories, and abilities into the classroom. Our responsibility is to create the conditions in which they can explore, communicate, collaborate, and grow.

Our shared direction

Belief serves mission.

How we see children and learning must help us build confident, capable, excited lifelong learners who can succeed holistically.

Eight principles

How we see children and learning

01

Child as protagonist

Children are at the centre of the story. Every employee works for children by helping provide a safe, tidy school with thoughtful, well-planned services and experiences.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Plan routines, services, and learning experiences around what benefits students.
  • Prepare safe, clean, organised spaces before students arrive.
  • Place a child's need for help above adult convenience or casual conversation.
  • Ask, “How does this decision serve the children?”
02

Child as collaborator

We believe in social constructivism: children learn best through collaboration. They build understanding by sharing ideas, exploring, solving problems, and creating with others.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Create regular opportunities for pairs and groups to investigate and create together.
  • Teach students to listen, take turns, negotiate, and build on one another's ideas.
  • Guide conflict without taking over the problem-solving process.
  • Collaborate across staff roles so students experience consistent support.
03

Child as communicator

Children have hundreds of languages: many ways to think, express, create, question, and connect. Children must feel safe and seen to strengthen their voice.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Invite students to communicate through words, movement, drawing, building, music, play, and technology.
  • Stop, look, listen, and respond when a student speaks or asks for help.
  • Give students time and emotional safety to express an idea.
  • Never silence, shame, mock, dismiss, or diminish a student's voice.
04

Environment as teacher

Our spaces have identity, purpose, order, beauty, and opportunities for exploration. Shared school spaces are for students.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Prepare accessible materials that invite investigation and independent use.
  • Keep classrooms and shared areas safe, tidy, purposeful, and ready for learning.
  • Value student work and make the learning process visible in the space.
  • Treat spaces as belonging to students unless clearly marked staff-only.
05

Teacher as guide

We learn alongside children. Teachers observe, listen, question, support, and create opportunities for discovery.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Observe before stepping in or providing an answer.
  • Use open questions, choices, prompts, and materials to extend thinking.
  • Support students to attempt, revise, and solve problems independently.
  • Redirect calmly and describe the behaviour you want to see.
06

Teacher as researcher

We learn about our students so that we can better support their learning. We observe, listen, document, reflect, and adapt.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Observe closely and record meaningful questions, interests, strengths, and patterns.
  • Use evidence rather than assumptions when discussing a student.
  • Reflect with colleagues and adjust plans in response to what students show us.
  • Document concerns and speak with the HoD before recommending outside intervention.
07

Documentation

We make learning visible so students, teachers, and families can understand and reflect on the learning journey.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Capture the process of learning, not only the finished product.
  • Use student words, work samples, observations, and appropriate photographs.
  • Review documentation with students to encourage reflection and next steps.
  • Share documentation purposefully while protecting privacy and confidentiality.
08

Parents as partners

Learning connects home and school. Families and educators bring different knowledge that helps us support the whole child.

See in practice
What this looks like at Gardner
  • Communicate warmly, clearly, consistently, and with the student at the centre.
  • Listen to what families know about their child and share factual observations.
  • Ask internally before answering when you are unsure about school information.
  • Maintain professional boundaries, privacy, and confidentiality.

02 — How we teach

We guide.
We don't control.

A Gardner educator observes, listens, questions, supports, and creates opportunities for discovery. We help children develop the skills to make good choices independently.

Say what you want to see

Tell children what to do, not only what to stop doing.

Instead ofTry
Don't run.Use walking feet inside.
Don't shout.Use your classroom voice.
Stop talking.Let's listen to Maya.
Don't grab.Ask for a turn.
Be quiet!Let's lower our voices.

Stop & listen.

If you are speaking with another staff member, pause the adult conversation and help the student. If you are already helping another student, acknowledge the child and say, “I see you. I will be with you in a moment.”

Speak calmly.

Correction never requires shouting, threats, humiliation, or harsh language.

Positive Discipline Policy

Describe. Don't label.

Observe what happened, describe the behaviour, and focus on what the child is learning next.

Model language.

Use clear, proper English and communication students should be encouraged to imitate.

A Gardner non-negotiable

We never diminish a child.

We never diminish a child’s efforts, feelings, or voice, especially their voice. We listen seriously, respond respectfully, and correct without force, humiliation, or degradation. A capable child is formed through confidence: children become capable when the adults around them protect their dignity and believe in what they can become.

Engaged teaching

Creativity and engagement are part of the work.

Make every reasonable effort to design learning that invites curiosity, participation, imagination, and meaningful challenge. Honour Gardner’s practice of differentiated learning and use Bloom’s Taxonomy to move students from remembering and understanding towards applying, analysing, evaluating, and creating.

Engaged Teaching Policy
01Invite participation

Begin with meaningful questions, materials, experiences, and choices.

02Differentiate the pathway

Adjust support, challenge, process, and expression for how students learn.

03Move thinking forward

Plan opportunities to apply, analyse, evaluate, and create.

The environment teaches

Intentional. Inviting. Organised. Beautiful. Accessible.

The school and all its learning areas are for students, not staff. Gardner staff are stewards of these spaces: we prepare them, maintain them, and guide students as they learn within them. They are not our personal spaces unless they are clearly designated as staff-only areas. Materials should invite exploration, student work should be valued, and every shared area should support communication, collaboration, and learning.

Relevant to current learningArtwork, posters, and visual materials displayed on walls must connect to what students are actively learning. Walls are learning tools, not space for general decoration.

Children’s work leadsMore than 50% of available wall-display space must be devoted to children’s work and documentation of their learning.

03 — How we show up

Be someone others can depend on.

Students, colleagues, and families depend on us. Professionalism at Gardner is active, prepared, reliable, and ready for the work of learning.

Dress for learning.

Choose comfortable professional clothing and flat, closed-toe shoes suitable for sitting, painting, building, gardening, investigating, running, and playing.

Dress Code Policy

Every day matters.

Learning does not stop because assessments are finished or the term is almost over. Every school day offers opportunities to explore, practise, create, and grow.

Be dependable.

Arrive on time, be prepared, be where you are assigned, communicate when something prevents you from working, and follow through.

04 — How we build relationships

Parents are partners.

School, family, and child are on the same team. Partnership requires trust, consistency, communication, and respect.

SchoolFamilyChild

We speak as one school.

Every conversation with a parent is held as a representative of Gardner Academy, never in a personal capacity. Communicate constructively about the school and encourage its efforts. Ask internally before answering when you are unsure.

Frustration, disagreement, or discontent must be raised through the appropriate internal supervision, management, grievance, or safeguarding channel and never discussed with parents.

Family Communication Policy

Partnership needs boundaries.

Keep money, gifts, personal businesses, confidential information, and school relationships professionally separate.

Gifts & Hospitality Policy

We model collaboration.

Speak respectfully, ask questions, give and receive feedback, support colleagues, and address problems rather than gossiping.

Workplace Conflict Policy

Communicating with parents

Every message should strengthen partnership.

Whether the message shares good news, a reminder, a concern, or a request, write as a representative of Gardner Academy. Be warm and direct, use factual observations, and make it clear how school and family can support the child together.

  1. 01
    Greet.

    Use the parent or guardian’s name and begin personally and professionally. Avoid overly casual openings, group-chat language, or sending a message without context.

  2. 02
    Connect.

    Begin with a truthful positive observation or shared purpose. This is not an empty compliment; it shows that the child is known and that the conversation begins from care.

  3. 03
    Inform.

    State clearly what the family needs to know. Describe what was observed, when it happened, and what support was provided. Use facts rather than labels, blame, assumptions, or emotionally charged language.

  4. 04
    Return to the student.

    Explain the learning, wellbeing, or developmental need behind the message. Share the next step at school and invite information or support from home. The child, not adult frustration, remains the centre.

  5. 05
    Close sincerely.

    End with appreciation and partnership. Make any requested action or follow-up clear, sign off in your school role, and use an approved communication channel.

Example message

Good afternoon, Ms Ndlovu.

Lebo brought wonderful ideas to our group story today and was eager to contribute.

During the discussion, he found it difficult to wait for his turn and needed several reminders to listen while classmates were speaking.

We are helping Lebo practise sharing his ideas while also making space for other voices. We will continue using a visual turn-taking cue at school. Please let us know if there is a strategy that works well at home.

Thank you for partnering with us to support Lebo’s confidence and listening skills.

Kind regards,
Ms Moyo
Gardner Academy

Family Communication Policy

When you do not know

Do not guess.

Check with the appropriate staff member and follow up when promised. Never present a personal assumption as the school’s position.

“I’m not sure. I’ll find out and get back to you.”

05 — How we care

When children are in our care, they have our attention.

Supervision is active. Being physically present is not enough. We position ourselves, pay attention, and remain responsible until care is properly transferred.

01

Spread out.

Position yourself where you can see students. Supervision time is not socialising time.

02

Stay present.

Never leave students unattended. Remain until responsibility is properly transferred.

Safeguarding Policy
03

Stop & listen.

A child asking for help takes priority over a casual adult conversation.

04

Keep it private.

Do not discuss students, families, staff, or confidential matters where children can hear.

Confidentiality Policy
05

School tasks, not personal errands.

Students may complete safe, age-appropriate school responsibilities, such as collecting paper for the class or taking a note to administration. They must not be used for an adult’s personal convenience, such as fetching a water bottle, food, phone, bag, or other personal belongings.

06

Fit for duty.

Every staff member has a duty of care to every child. Staff must arrive and remain able to supervise, respond, and make sound decisions. Gardner is drug-free, alcohol-free, smoke-free, and vape-free.

Fit for Duty Policy

When a child’s safety may be affected

Act. Protect. Report.

A concern about a child’s safety is never something to postpone, investigate alone, or manage informally.

Safeguarding Policy
  1. 01

    Act within your role. Respond immediately to reduce danger and make the situation as safe as possible.

  2. 02

    Protect the child’s voice. Listen calmly. Do not lead, dismiss, investigate, or promise secrecy.

  3. 03

    Report promptly. Use the safeguarding or management channel and record only factual information.

The Gardner Test

When you're unsure, begin with the child.

Policies cannot anticipate every situation. Use these questions to guide your judgement.

Mission & vision check

Does this move Gardner towards its purpose?

Good judgement is not only about avoiding harm. It should actively build the learner experience and graduate outcome we promise.

Review Mission & Vision
  • Does it build confidence and capability?
  • Does it create genuine excitement for learning?
  • Does it support holistic success, not only academic performance?
  • Is it good for the child?
  • Is it safe?
  • Does it treat the child as capable?
  • Does it support learning?
  • Does it strengthen partnership with families?
  • Would I be comfortable explaining this decision?
If you're still unsure Ask before acting.
